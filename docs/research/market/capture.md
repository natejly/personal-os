# Capture products: meeting notes, activity memory, and macOS dictation (as of 2026-10-02)

Scope is shipped product behavior and documented privacy defaults. No Grain audit. No accuracy benchmarks: third-party “percent accuracy” tables from dictation vendors were discarded because they do not publish a reproducible method. Dates below are the source’s own date when one was stated.

## What do leading meeting-note apps ship for local versus cloud transcription, diarization, and note enhancement that the user accepts?

### Takeaway

The 2026 split is capture location, not where the model runs. Granola captures on the device with no meeting bot, streams audio to a transcription provider, deletes that audio, and writes an enhanced note from the transcript plus the user’s own notes, which the user can edit, cite, and regenerate. Otter and Fireflies process audio in the cloud, keep recordings unless a retention rule deletes them, and diarize speakers; their “notes” are generated summaries, outlines, and action items the user can edit or regenerate while a transcript still exists. None of these three documents an on-device transcription mode.

### Cited Findings

**Granola — botless capture, cloud transcription, audio not retained**

- Granola runs on the device and captures microphone plus system audio. No bot joins. Desktop capture requires the macOS, Windows, iPhone, or Android app; the web app only views and edits existing notes. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)
- Transcription starts only after the user opens that meeting’s note, clicks a notification, clicks New Note, or is already in an upcoming meeting note (then it starts at the scheduled start). It does not transcribe every calendar event by itself. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)
- It passes audio “directly from your microphone and system audio to our transcription provider.” It “does not record or save audio or video at any point during the call.” It cannot transcribe an uploaded file. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)
- Audio is “temporarily cached during the meeting for transcription only” and deleted from Granola and third-party systems once transcription completes. The same deletion claim is repeated for iPhone cache. — [Security, Privacy & Data FAQs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs); [Getting Consent](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/getting-consent)
- It cannot isolate one app’s audio. Any system audio, including music, is in the transcript. Live transcript UI splits channels: grey bubbles are system audio (other people), green bubbles are the microphone. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)
- Stop is manual, or automatic when it decides the call ended, after 15 minutes with no new audio, or when the computer sleeps. Back-to-back calls can merge into one transcript if the meeting app keeps the mic open. On macOS below 14.2, non-admin users may not get the call-ended signal. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)
- Notes and transcripts sit on AWS in the United States, encrypted in transit and at rest, and are also cached on device. No EU, UK, or other residency option is offered. Retention is indefinite unless a policy is set. Enterprise can auto-delete transcripts; notes remain, but regeneration and chat that depended on the transcript degrade. — [Security, Privacy & Data FAQs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs); [Transcript auto-deletion](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/transcript-auto-deletion)
- Notes are private by default (only the user). Other levels are company-email holders, or anyone with the link. Admins cannot read private notes; they can cap sharing (for example, block public links). — [Security, Privacy & Data FAQs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)
- Model training: on Free and Business, anonymised data may be used for Granola’s own models unless the user turns off “Use my data to improve models for everyone.” Enterprise admins can opt the org out. “Third parties like OpenAI and Anthropic are never allowed to train on your data.” Opt-out is not retroactive. — [Security, Privacy & Data FAQs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)
- Enhanced notes use “a blend of different models, primarily OpenAI and Anthropic.” — [AI-enhanced notes](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/ai-enhanced-notes)
- SOC 2 Type II is current; ISO 27001 is not. HIPAA and a BAA are Enterprise-only and, per the FAQ, cover desktop and laptop apps only, not Free, Personal, or Business. Not FERPA. No first-party MFA; sign-in is Google, Microsoft, or Enterprise SSO. — [Security, Privacy & Data FAQs](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/security-privacy-data-faqs)
- Consent is the user’s legal duty. Product aids: video watermark while transcribing, and an automatic chat notification. Enterprise copy says conversation data is never used to train shared AI models. — [Getting Consent](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/consent-security-privacy/getting-consent); [Granola for Enterprise](https://www.granola.ai/enterprise)

**Granola — diarization is channel split plus platform names, not a general voice model**

- Named speaker tags using each participant’s display name exist for Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams on macOS and Windows. Without that, the transcript is “Me” and “Them” (mic vs system audio). The docs say the AI “usually” infers speakers from context, and the user can correct a name in the notes or ask chat to update it. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)
- The phone app “can also recognize different speakers during face-to-face meetings.” The iOS transcription page describes a single active mic (built-in or Bluetooth), not a per-app loopback. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription); [iOS transcription](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/ios/transcription)
- Personal jargon and workspace jargon are the documented accuracy lever, plus mic level and a headset. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)

**Granola — enhancement the user can accept, edit, or regenerate**

- When the call ends or the user stops, enhanced notes are generated from three inputs: the transcript, raw notes the user typed, and calendar-event information. Raw notes are highlighted in black. A magnifying glass on a point shows the transcript or raw-note span it came from. Chat (Cmd-J / Ctrl-J) can be asked about quotes. — [AI-enhanced notes](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/ai-enhanced-notes)
- Granola’s own blog states the review cue more sharply: points the user wrote stay black; detail Granola adds is grey. The user can edit everything. — [How to get the best from Granola](https://www.granola.ai/blog/get-the-best-from-granola)
- Acceptance is edit, not a separate approve button in the docs. The user can edit the enhanced note directly, or edit the raw notes and re-enhance. Chat accepts instructions such as “make the next steps section more detailed.” Templates can be swapped or regenerated. Built-in templates exist; users can write their own prompt and structure. — [AI-enhanced notes](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/ai-enhanced-notes)
- Edits do not change how later meetings are written. Style consistency is templates or profile settings, not per-note memory. Blank raw notes are supported: enhancement can run from transcript and calendar alone. Headers and bullets in raw notes are described as steering structure. — [AI-enhanced notes](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/ai-enhanced-notes); [Writing your own notes on mobile](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/ios/taking-notes)
- The user can resume transcription on a finished note and regenerate so new audio is included. Individual transcript chunks can be copied or deleted before that. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription)

**Otter — cloud ASR, stored audio, voice enrollment**

- Terms: the service turns conversations into notes with proprietary ASR, keyword extraction, and “voice to text synchronization.” Audio may arrive from the mobile or web app, an upload, or a video-conferencing sync. “Audio is processed in cloud infrastructure” and can later be downloaded. — [Otter.ai Terms of Service](https://otter.ai/terms-of-service)
- Privacy policy collects audio recordings and, for virtual meetings, automatic OtterPilot screenshots “to add value to the meetings by extracting useful visual information.” Speaker-identification information is generated so Otter can tag names. Cloud storage named in the policy includes Amazon Web Services in the United States. — [Otter.ai Privacy Policy](https://otter.ai/privacy-policy)
- The same policy lists a purpose of improving the service “including training our proprietary AI technology on de-identified audio recordings and on transcriptions (which may contain Personal Information),” with cloud, labeling, and “artificial intelligence service providers,” under consent or legitimate interests. — [Otter.ai Privacy Policy](https://otter.ai/privacy-policy)
- Deleting a conversation unshares it immediately, holds it in Trash for 30 days, then permanently deletes it from Otter’s servers. Deletion does not refund transcription minutes. — [Delete a conversation](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360047733414-Delete-a-conversation)
- Enterprise admins can strip audio and leave the transcript, hide transcript or summary from end users, and (via Otter, not a self-serve toggle) disable export. Import of files can be disabled on web and desktop; the help article says Otter does not enforce that on the mobile app. — [Enterprise Admin Controls Overview](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/13352505516695-Enterprise-Admin-Controls-Overview)
- Enterprise retention can be set separately for audio/video, for the transcript, or for both. After the transcript retention window, the summary cannot be regenerated. Objects listed as conversation data include transcript, audio/video, summary, action items, outline, highlights, comments, images, insights, generated template content, and speaker tags. The help example uses a November 2025 conversation, so the article is current relative to this review. — [Set a custom Data Retention policy](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/19500988656279-Set-a-custom-Data-Retention-policy)
- Enterprise public API can retrieve transcripts, audio, action items, insights, and outlines. — [Otter.ai Public API](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/36130822688279-Otter-ai-Public-API)

**Otter — diarization plus learned speaker identity**

- Diarization splits the transcript into speaker turns (Speaker 1, Speaker 2, or Unknown). A separate “speaker enrollment” store of voice data can auto-label people across conversations. A workspace setting can disable that enrollment. Labels then come from the video platform’s participant names or from manual tags. Manual labels are never overwritten. With enrollment off, Otter “will not attempt to cross-reference past recordings.” — [Disable Speaker Learning](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/40643231903639-Disable-Speaker-Learning)
- Identification runs after the recording stops or an import finishes. Tagging one “Speaker #” retags the other turns of that voice in the conversation and is described as teaching future conversations. Live auto-tagging from Zoom participant names is documented for Otter Notetaker, not for every source. — [Speaker Identification Overview](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/21665587209367-Speaker-Identification-Overview)
- Best-practices article, updated 20 January 2026: users should tag “Speaker 1 / Unknown,” keep one speaker per paragraph, and may import old audio and label it so later identification improves. — [Best Practices to Maximize Speaker Identification](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/37817248501783-Best-Practices-to-Maximize-Speaker-Identification)
- Speaker management (merge duplicates, rename, hide) applies across past and future transcripts in the account. The help article describes a gradual rollout. — [Speaker Management](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/40586357592471-Speaker-Management)

**Otter — generated notes the user keeps beside the transcript**

- Retention and admin docs treat summary, action items, outline, insights, highlights, and “generated meeting template content” as first-class outputs separate from the transcript. Regenerating a summary requires the transcript. — [Set a custom Data Retention policy](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/19500988656279-Set-a-custom-Data-Retention-policy)
- Usage information in the privacy policy includes edit events, so editing is an expected action, but this review did not find an Otter page that describes an accept/reject diff or a black/grey author split like Granola’s. — [Otter.ai Privacy Policy](https://otter.ai/privacy-policy)

**Fireflies — meeting bot, cloud processing, vendor zero-retention, Fireflies keeps the result**

- The product joins calls as a bot. A participant email opt-out stops the bot from joining if any one participant opts out. The host can remove the bot from the dashboard or the meeting app. A company blog says removal within 3 minutes means the meeting is not captured; that timing is blog copy, not the privacy policy. — [Ensuring Meeting Compliance with Fireflies AI Notetaker](https://fireflies.ai/blog/meeting-compliance-with-fireflies/)
- User content, in the company’s security FAQ, includes transcription, summaries, audio and video recordings, AskFred chats, and soundbites. The user owns it; Fireflies describes itself as custodian. That FAQ also says data is “saved for at least 12 months” unless the user deletes a meeting (immediate) or the account (within 30 days). Treat the “12 months” line as possibly stale: the privacy policy says account personal information is kept while the account is active and deleted within 30 days of closure, and the knowledge base says orgs can auto-delete recordings, transcripts, and notes after a chosen period. — [The top 15 questions on Fireflies security](https://fireflies.ai/blog/the-top-15-frequently-asked-questions-about-fireflies-security-2/); [Privacy Policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy); [Responsible and secure meeting notetaking](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/7434774675-responsible-and-secure-meeting-notetaking-with-fireflies)
- Privacy policy “Zero Data Retention” for meeting content (audio, video, transcripts, summaries, and Fireflies Talk): third-party vendors do not store it after processing, do not keep access after the service completes, and do not use it to train internal or external models. The policy also says Fireflies does not collect, use, or sell personal data to train large language models. This is vendor retention, not “Fireflies deletes the user’s copy.” — [Privacy Policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy); [How Fireflies.ai keeps your information safe](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/2154538358-policy-on-keeping-information-safe)
- The security FAQ says 256-bit AES at rest and TLS 1.2 in transit, a BAA with OpenAI plus OpenAI zero-data-retention, and an optional Private Storage bucket. The knowledge base states encryption in transit and at rest and claims GDPR, HIPAA, FERPA, and CCPA support via retention controls, without repeating the AES bit length. — [The top 15 questions on Fireflies security](https://fireflies.ai/blog/the-top-15-frequently-asked-questions-about-fireflies-security-2/); [Responsible and secure meeting notetaking](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/7434774675-responsible-and-secure-meeting-notetaking-with-fireflies)
- Knowledge base: internal staff do not access meeting content by default; access needs the customer’s permission. Recording choice sits with the user or host. Sharing can be password-protected with an expiry. Enterprise has a rules engine for recording, transcription, and retention. — [How Fireflies.ai keeps your information safe](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/2154538358-policy-on-keeping-information-safe)

**Fireflies — speaker labels and regenerating notes after a rename**

- Marketing API page: speaker names for Google Meet and Zoom; other platforms are “Speaker 1, Speaker 2.” — [Fireflies.ai API](https://fireflies.ai/api)
- The transcript API returns `speakers` (id, name) and per-sentence `speaker_name` / `speaker_id`, plus a summary object (keywords, action items, outline, overview, bullets, topics, chapters) and `audio_url` / `video_url`. — [Transcript query](https://docs.fireflies.ai/graphql-api/query/transcript)
- Uploaded files are diarized as Speaker 1, 2, 3. The meeting owner can rename one turn or every turn of that speaker. Renames do not alter the audio. The knowledge base says to regenerate notes afterward so summaries and action items pick up the new names. — [How to Edit Speaker Labels in Uploaded Files](https://guide.fireflies.ai/articles/1234873612-how-to-edit-speaker-labels-in-uploaded-files)

**Apple Notes as the OS meeting-transcription bound (not a third-party notes app)**

- At WWDC25 (June 2025), Apple said Notes already recorded and transcribed phone calls, live audio, and recorded audio, and that those features were integrated with Apple Intelligence summaries. They said `SpeechTranscriber` was built for that quality bar: fast, accurate at longer distances, and on device. — [Bring advanced speech-to-text to your app with SpeechAnalyzer (WWDC25)](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/)
- This review did not re-fetch the 2026 Notes user guide, so live-transcript UX, language list, and whether the summary step is on-device Apple Intelligence or Private Cloud Compute are not restated here.

### Inferences

- “Local meeting notes” in this set means local capture and local control of the note, not local ASR. Granola’s privacy story is “no bot, no retained audio,” while the transcript and the enhanced note still depend on a cloud transcription provider and on OpenAI/Anthropic.
- Speaker identity has three documented mechanisms, and products mix them: (1) mic versus system-audio channels (Granola desktop default), (2) the video platform’s participant names (Granola on Meet/Zoom/Teams, Otter Zoom Notetaker, Fireflies on Meet/Zoom), (3) acoustic diarization plus optional voice enrollment (Otter by default; Fireflies uploads as Speaker N). A desktop app that only taps mic and system audio cannot name a third remote speaker from audio alone without (2) or (3).
- The enhancement pattern a user is shown how to accept is Granola’s: keep the user’s words, attach model text, cite the transcript, and allow regenerate. Otter and Fireflies ship the summary as a stored artifact the user edits; the transcript is the source of truth required to regenerate.
- Training defaults are not aligned. Otter’s policy still describes training on de-identified audio and on transcripts. Granola’s default on Free/Business is opt-out for its own models, with a contractual ban on OpenAI and Anthropic training. Fireflies’ policy forbids training on meeting content, including by its vendors.

### Gaps

- No official page fetched for Fathom, tl;dv, Fellow, Zoom AI Companion, or Google Meet’s note-taking product. Those are likely part of a full market set; they are absent here, not judged.
- Notion AI meeting notes were not fetched from Notion’s own guide in this pass.
- Otter’s help center does not, in the pages read, state whether OtterPilot is the only capture path or whether a botless desktop loopback exists. The terms describe mobile/web recording, upload, and conferencing sync.
- Granola does not name the transcription provider. “Our transcription provider” is the extent of the doc.
- Apple’s October 2026 Notes transcription language list and summary privacy path were not fetched.
- Fireflies’ “saved for at least 12 months” blog line conflicts with user-controlled deletion in the privacy policy and knowledge base. Which clock actually runs was not resolved past that conflict.

## What do activity or computer-use memory products ship, and what privacy defaults do they document?

### Takeaway

Still-shipping activity memory in 2026 is local-first and opt-in at the capture layer, with very different payloads. ActivityWatch logs app and website use into a local database and never uploads it. Screenpipe stores screen, audio, and related text on the machine unless the user turns on a cloud path. Windows Recall saves periodic screenshots only after an explicit opt-in, analyzes them locally, and does not record audio. Limitless’s continuous Mac screen-and-audio product (Rewind) had capture turned off on 19 December 2025 after the Meta acquisition; the Pendant and its cloud audio retention settings are what the December 2025 policy still describes.

### Cited Findings

**ActivityWatch — local app/website time tracking, not screen or audio memory**

- It is an open-source automated time tracker for Windows, macOS, Linux, and Android that “monitors which applications and websites you use.” The FAQ contrasts it with cloud time trackers: data stays on the device and is not sent to a server. Storage is a SQLite database. Export, delete, and backup are local. — [FAQ](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/faq.html)
- Privacy policy: collected data is “local computer usage data”; it is stored only on the device; the developers have no access; nothing is transmitted to external servers; there is no third-party analytics. The only network behavior called out is an HTTP check for GitHub releases, which can be disabled. The policy does not claim encryption. — [Privacy Policy](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/privacy.html)
- macOS data path example: `~/Library/Application Support/activitywatch/aw-server-rust`. — [Directories](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/directories.html)
- Security doc: the app is only as secure as the host. Malware or anyone who can read the database file can read the data. There is no API authentication, so it is “not secure on systems with multiple users.” CORS allows localhost and the browser extension origin; on Firefox that origin is per-install, which the project itself says lets a malicious extension read the datastore. Encrypting old data is described as future work (“the easiest way to build this would be…”), not as a shipped default. — [Security](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/security.html)

**Screenpipe — local screen, audio, and text, cloud only if enabled**

- Docs: screen, audio, input, browser metadata, meeting transcripts, and connected-app context are stored on the machine unless the user enables a cloud provider, sync/archive, or an external integration. Default location given as `~/.screenpipe/`. Screen and audio capture, local search, and chat with local Ollama are marked as not leaving the machine. Chat with a cloud model, cloud pipes, a connected-app proxy, cloud media analysis, an enclave PII filter, cloud archive, and a support log bundle do leave, and only when that feature is used. — [Privacy data flow](https://docs.screenpipe.com/privacy-data-flow)
- Security page: capture and storage stay local; “AI is the only egress path, and you pick the mode.” Local mode is described as Screenpipe never receiving client content, so they are not a processor for it. Hosted AI and Enterprise sync are the paths that make them one. The page says Screenpipe does not sell screen content, and that SOC-style claims are internal programs rather than third-party certifications. — [Security](https://screenpipe.com/security)
- Architecture page: local API requires a bearer token even for localhost (no localhost bypass). Secrets in the local SQLite database are AES-256-GCM with the key in the OS keychain. The page identifies a 2026 security-review revision. — [Security Architecture](https://screenpipe.com/security/architecture)
- Privacy filter, before selected AI workflows: local text redaction, local image redaction, or a confidential enclave (text leaves the device for an attested no-disk enclave). Cloud media analysis can be turned off so cloud audio, video, and image analysis is blocked. A chat “shield” adds `filter_pii=1` to searches. — [Privacy filter](https://docs.screenpipe.com/privacy-filter)
- The marketing privacy-filter page describes the enclave as hosted by Tinfoil, with CPU memory encryption and a published measurement the client can check. That is an optional path, not the default capture path. — [Privacy filter (product page)](https://screenpipe.com/privacy-filter)

**Microsoft Recall — Windows, opt-in screenshots, no audio, local analysis**

- Recall is a Copilot+ PC feature. Snapshots are off until the user opts in, either in Settings > Privacy & security > Recall & snapshots or on first launch. Snapshots are periodic screenshots plus text derived locally with OCR. Recall “doesn’t record audio or save continuous video,” and does not save game video while Game Mode is active on supported platforms. — [Privacy and control over your Recall experience](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/privacy/privacy-and-control-over-your-recall-experience); [Retrace your steps with Recall](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-features/retrace-your-steps-with-recall)
- Microsoft’s support pages say no internet or cloud connection is required or used to save and analyze snapshots. Snapshots are not shared with Microsoft, with third parties, or with other Windows users on the same device. Diagnostic data can still flow under the user’s existing Windows diagnostics settings. — [Privacy and control over your Recall experience](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/privacy/privacy-and-control-over-your-recall-experience)
- Launch and access require Windows Hello with at least one biometric (face or fingerprint). Snapshots and the search index are encrypted; keys are protected by the TPM and tied to Windows Hello Enhanced Sign-in Security, decrypted in a VBS enclave. IT can disable Recall by policy but cannot turn snapshot saving on for the user. — [Manage Recall](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/client-management/Manage-Recall)
- Sensitive-information filtering is on by default and runs on device with the NPU and the Microsoft Classification Engine. Snapshots are not saved when it flags sensitive content. Users can also exclude apps and websites. Supported-browser filtering applies to foreground-tab content. DRM content is not stored, same as the Snipping Tool. Apps can mark a window so Recall stores it blank (`WDA_MONITOR`). — [Filtering apps, websites, and sensitive information in Recall](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/filtering-apps-websites-and-sensitive-information-in-recall-a4c28bee-e200-4a4a-b60d-c0522b404a5b); [Recall overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/recall/)
- Microsoft Learn’s overview timeline lists general availability of Recall on Copilot+ PCs on 25 April 2025. This is a Windows feature, not a Mac feature. — [Recall overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/recall/)

**Limitless / Rewind — screen capture discontinued; Pendant cloud audio remains in the Dec 2025 policy**

- On 5 December 2025, 9to5Mac reported Limitless (formerly Rewind) had been acquired by Meta. The latest app update would disable all screen and audio capture starting 19 December 2025. Existing Pendant customers would be supported at least another year, the Pendant would not be sold to new customers, and continuing customers would not pay a subscription. Non-Pendant functionality such as Rewind would be sunset. Regional availability changed the same day, with a 19 December 2025 deadline to download data in regions where the service was ending. The article is a news report of the company’s email, not the company’s own post. — [Rewind Mac app shutting down following Meta acquisition](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/)
- The privacy policy’s “last updated” line is 5 December 2025, the same day as that announcement. It still covers the website, app, and Pendant. Pendant users “have the ability” to choose retention of audio recordings and information derived from them, “including the ability to retain such information indefinitely.” — [Limitless Privacy Policy](https://www.limitless.ai/privacy-policy)
- The product privacy page (still published at fetch time) says: Apple Accessory Kit for authentication and encrypted phone transfer; TLS in transit; storage with hardware security modules; download or delete from the app and API; audio retention choices of 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 1 year, or forever; full recordings stay until the user deletes them or closes the account; the user can stop recording and delete any recording; users must proactively notify and get consent, and that duty is also in the terms. — [Limitless privacy](https://www.limitless.ai/privacy)
- Help center articles still document Rewind’s local data at `~/Library/Application Support/com.memoryvault.MemoryVault` and a Settings > Storage > “Delete all data” path, plus removing the login item. They say there is no supported API for that Rewind data. “Updated over 3 weeks ago” is the help center’s relative stamp at crawl time, so the uninstall docs were still being maintained after the shutdown announcement. — [Where can I find my Rewind data?](https://help.limitless.ai/en/articles/13048802-where-can-i-find-my-rewind-data); [How do I delete / uninstall Rewind?](https://help.limitless.ai/en/articles/13024699-how-do-i-delete-uninstall-rewind)

**Meeting products that also grab pixels, narrowly**

- Otter’s privacy policy says OtterPilot may take automatic screenshots during virtual meetings and attach them to the transcript to pull visual information. That is meeting-scoped screen capture inside a cloud notes product, not an all-day activity log. — [Otter.ai Privacy Policy](https://otter.ai/privacy-policy)

### Inferences

- Documented defaults for “computer memory” in 2026, where the product still exists, are: do not upload by default, and do not start continuous capture without a user action (ActivityWatch is the exception on start: it is a background tracker, but its payload is window/web usage, not pixels or mic audio).
- Recall is the clearest OS-vendor pattern: off until opt-in, screenshots rather than video or audio, sensitive-content filter on, biometric gate, IT can forbid but cannot force capture.
- Rewind-class always-on screen recording is not a live Mac product from Limitless as of the December 2025 shutdown. Comparing against Rewind’s old behavior without that date would be stale.
- Screenpipe is the still-documented Mac-relevant product that records screen and audio locally and treats cloud AI as an explicit egress. Its privacy filter is about what an AI is allowed to read, not about whether capture happened.

### Gaps

- No 2026 Limitless policy newer than 5 December 2025 was found. Whether Pendant support was still live on 2 October 2026, past the “at least another year” promise, was not confirmed from a primary page.
- The full country list in the regional shutdown email was truncated in the news excerpt and is not repeated here.
- ActivityWatch watcher-level detail (exact window-title fields, browser URL scope) was not fetched beyond “applications and websites” and the component names in the security and directories docs.
- No official page was fetched for other always-on Mac memory products that might have launched after Rewind’s shutdown.

## What is table stakes for dictation into a notes editor on macOS in 2026?

### Takeaway

The operating-system floor, in the current Mac User Guide, is cursor insertion in any text field, a shortcut, optional on-device processing for ordinary dictation, typing while speaking on Apple silicon, no length cap, auto-punctuation, and a few spoken formatting commands. Third-party dictation apps treat that as the base and add a global hotkey, automatic paste into the focused app, a personal vocabulary, and a rewrite step (modes or a cloud model) that turns speech into sendable prose. Wispr Flow documents that this rewrite path’s transcription always runs in the cloud. Superwhisper documents an offline path that “only run[s] really well” on Apple silicon, with cloud models as the Intel recommendation.

### Cited Findings

**Apple Dictation — current Mac User Guide**

- The guide’s contents list “What’s new in macOS 27,” so the unversioned page fetched for this review is the current guide, not the macOS 14 snapshot. Dictation “speak[s] to enter text anywhere you can type it.” — [Dictate messages and documents on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/mac)
- On-device versus server is a setting the user is told to read, not a single global rule in this article: Keyboard settings text under Dictation states whether “voice inputs and transcripts for general text Dictation (for example, composing messages and notes, but not dictating in a search box) are processed on your device and not sent to Siri servers.” — [Dictate messages and documents on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/mac)
- Enabling Dictation asks whether to share audio recordings so Apple may store and review a sample. The user can choose “Share Audio Recordings” or “Not Now,” and can later toggle Improve Siri & Dictation under Privacy & Security. — [Dictate messages and documents on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/mac)
- Start via the microphone key, a customizable shortcut, or Edit > Start Dictation. On Apple silicon the user can type without stopping dictation; the highlight pauses while they type. Length is unlimited. Dictation stops on Escape, the shortcut, the mic key, or 30 seconds of silence. — [Dictate messages and documents on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/mac)
- Spoken commands insert emoji by name, punctuation by name, “new line,” and “new paragraph.” In supported languages, commas, periods, and question marks are inserted automatically; Auto-punctuation can be turned off. Ambiguous words are underlined in blue and offer alternatives. — [Dictate messages and documents on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/mac)
- Multiple dictation languages can be installed and switched from the cursor or the Globe key. Microphone source can be set, or left on Automatic. Voice Control replaces standard Dictation while Voice Control is on. Availability varies by language and region; the article points at the macOS Feature Availability page rather than listing languages. — [Dictate messages and documents on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/mac)
- The macOS 14 guide is more absolute and should be treated as stale relative to the current page: it said Apple silicon dictation for supported languages stays on device with no network, and that Intel Macs or languages without on-device support send utterances to Apple. The current page tells the user to read the Keyboard settings line instead of repeating that Intel rule. — [Dictate messages and documents on Mac (macOS 14 guide)](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/use-dictation-mh40584/14.0/mac/14.0)

**Wispr Flow — system-wide dictation whose transcription is cloud-only**

- Product privacy page: “Effortless voice dictation in every application.” “Transcription always happens in the cloud to provide the best speed and accuracy.” Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Certifications claimed: SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, HIPAA (BAA). They say they do not sell data. — [Wispr Flow privacy](https://wisprflow.ai/privacy)
- “Improve the model for everyone” (formerly Privacy Mode) chooses whether Wispr may use dictation audio, transcripts, and edits to evaluate, train, or improve models. It is separate from storage. — [Wispr Flow privacy](https://wisprflow.ai/privacy); [Data Controls](https://wisprflow.ai/data-controls)
- Dictation Cloud Storage chooses whether transcripts, audio, and dictation history stay on Wispr’s servers for cross-device features. Turning training off “does not stop cloud processing.” Notetaker transcripts are stored in Wispr’s cloud for sync and resume; help center says there is no customer setting to opt out of that Notetaker storage, though retention is configurable. A signed BAA locks both model improvement and Dictation Cloud Storage off. — [Data Controls](https://wisprflow.ai/data-controls); [Manage data sharing, cloud storage and local history](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/9609615338-private-cloud-sync-and-data-sharing-preferences-in-wispr-flow)
- Third-party AI providers used for dictation are under zero-data-retention agreements and cannot train on the data. Some Notetaker brief features use third-party features that are not under zero-data-retention; the data-controls page says those providers still cannot train on the data. — [Data Controls](https://wisprflow.ai/data-controls)
- Desktop local history is a third knob: store locally, auto-delete every 24 hours, or never store locally. It does not by itself change cloud processing or Notetaker storage. — [Understanding Privacy Mode and Private Cloud Sync](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4709791908-understanding-privacy-mode-and-cloud-sync)

**Superwhisper — offline on Apple silicon, paste into the focused app, modes that rewrite**

- Homepage: voice becomes “polished text” in Slack, Gmail, and “any other site or app.” “Works offline.” Custom vocabulary for names, abbreviations, and specialized terms, remembered after one entry. Predefined modes (Voice, Message, Email) set tone, structure, and formatting. The edited transcript is pasted into the app in use, without a manual copy. Custom shortcuts. File transcription for uploaded audio and video. Custom modes can include formatting rules and prompts. Model picker lists GPT, Claude, or Llama. Also marketed: meeting recording and transcription, “100+ languages,” and “unlimited use of Whisper models” on a paid feature list. Platforms include macOS, Windows, iOS, and Android. — [Superwhisper](https://superwhisper.com)
- FAQ on that page: Intel Macs “work best with Cloud models. Offline models only run really well on Apple Silicon macs.” Free tier after a 3,000-word Pro trial. Team plan lists SOC 2 Type II and “Enterprise hosted models” plus model access control. — [Superwhisper](https://superwhisper.com)
- The homepage does not say whether offline mode keeps audio off the network, or whether “polished text” always calls GPT/Claude. It does say offline transcription works, and that cloud models are the recommended path on Intel.

**A small open-source proof of the OS API as a dictation layer**

- Yap (source repo) targets macOS 26 on Apple silicon, uses `SpeechAnalyzer` on device, binds a global shortcut, and pastes into the focused field. The README says it makes no network calls, and that the project dropped Intel because the older recognizer can send audio to Apple. It contrasts itself with Apple Dictation by offering press-to-stop and a preview before insert. This is one app’s README, not Apple’s documentation. — [FrigadeHQ/yap](https://github.com/frigadehq/yap)

### Inferences

- Table stakes for typing into a notes editor, if “table stakes” means what a Mac already does with no extra app: insert at the caret, shortcut, auto-punctuation, newline/paragraph commands, and a visible on-device-versus-server status for ordinary prose. Apple does not document a prose-cleanup model, a personal jargon list, or per-app tone modes.
- Table stakes for the 2026 third-party dictation category, from the two vendor sites above: work in whatever app is focused, paste automatically, keep a vocabulary, and offer a formatting/rewrite mode. Privacy is a product choice inside that category, not a shared default. Wispr is cloud transcription by its own statement. Superwhisper sells offline Whisper-class models on Apple silicon and cloud LLMs as an option.
- “Edits” as a Wispr training field implies the user corrects dictated text and that correction is valuable. That is the acceptance loop for dictation, parallel to Granola’s edit-the-enhanced-note loop, but it is inline text, not a separate notes document.
- Comparison blogs that rank Wispr, Superwhisper, and MacWhisper with accuracy percentages were not used. They disagree with each other and several are published by a competing dictation app.

- Superwhisper’s docs site was not fetched, so default model (local Whisper versus cloud) for a new Apple silicon install is not known. The homepage only guarantees that offline mode exists and that Intel should use cloud models.
- The macOS Feature Availability language matrix for Dictation was not fetched, so the on-device language list as of 2 October 2026 is a gap.

## Which pieces can run on device (whisper-class, OS speech) versus requiring a cloud?

### Takeaway

Speech-to-text can run entirely on device in 2026: whisper.cpp does local Whisper inference, including on Apple silicon via Metal and Core ML, and Apple’s `SpeechTranscriber` (macOS 26 and the matching OS releases) is an on-device model aimed at long-form audio such as meetings. Ordinary Mac dictation can also stay on device when Keyboard settings say so. Diarization, and the note-enhancement step used by Granola, Otter, Fireflies, and Wispr’s Notetaker, are documented as cloud or as a separate optional local LLM, not as part of those on-device recognizers. Streaming meeting audio to a transcription vendor is a cloud requirement even when the vendor deletes the audio afterward.

### Cited Findings

**whisper.cpp — local Whisper, no network required at inference**

- The project is “high-performance inference of OpenAI’s Whisper” in C/C++, with Apple silicon called out as a first-class target via ARM NEON, Accelerate, Metal, and Core ML. CPU-only inference is supported. An iPhone sample is described as “fully offline, on-device.” On Apple silicon, “the inference runs fully on the GPU via Metal.” — [whisper.cpp README](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/blob/master/README.md)
- The CLI transcribes a 16-bit WAV. Other formats are converted first; the documented ffmpeg target is 16 kHz, mono, pcm_s16le. Models are downloaded in ggml format (the quick start uses `base.en`). — [whisper.cpp README](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/blob/master/README.md)
- Voice activity detection is a listed feature. A stream example does “real-time inference on audio from your microphone” by sampling about every half second and transcribing continuously; the README calls that example naive. — [whisper.cpp README](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/blob/master/README.md)
- Supported platforms in that README include Mac OS on Intel and Arm, iOS, Android, Linux, Windows, and WebAssembly. WebAssembly is local to the browser only in the sense that the model runs in the page; it is still the user’s machine.

**Apple Speech framework — two on-device transcribers, different jobs**

- `SpeechTranscriber` is “a speech-to-text transcription module that’s appropriate for normal conversation and general purposes.” Apps are told to check `isAvailable` and `supportedLocales`, and to fall back to `DictationTranscriber` when the device does not support it. Locales can be installed on device or downloaded. — [SpeechTranscriber](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechtranscriber)
- `DictationTranscriber` is “similar to system dictation features and compatible with older devices.” — [SpeechTranscriber](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechtranscriber)
- WWDC25: the new model does not ship inside the app binary or the app’s memory limit; the system stores it, shares it across apps, and installs updates. `SpeechTranscriber` is on device, meant to handle longer distances, and was the model Notes used. It is on all platforms except watchOS, with hardware requirements, and “can currently transcribe these languages, with more to come” (the talk does not list them in the fetched transcript text). `DictationTranscriber` matches the languages, model, and devices of the older on-device recognizer, and does not require the user to enable Siri or keyboard dictation for that language first. — [WWDC25 session 277](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/)
- Presets include a progressive live preset (`volatileResults` and `fastResults`) and time-indexed presets that attach audio time ranges. That is the API shape for a live transcript with timestamps, still on device when `SpeechTranscriber` is the module. — [SpeechTranscriber.Preset](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechtranscriber/preset)
- A third-party guide dated around September 2026 claims `SpeechTranscriber` covered ten languages on macOS 26.6 and that the older `SFSpeechRecognizer` can be forced on-device with `requiresOnDeviceRecognition`, at a documented accuracy cost, while some languages still need the network. That language count is not from Apple and may already be stale by 2 October 2026. — [Apple's SpeechAnalyzer (Notey)](https://noteymeet.com/guides/apple-speechanalyzer-macos-26); [On-device transcription (Notey)](https://noteymeet.com/guides/on-device-transcription-explained)

**What the meeting and dictation products say must be cloud**

- Granola: live audio goes to “our transcription provider”; enhanced notes are primarily OpenAI and Anthropic. Audio is not kept. There is no documented on-device ASR option. — [How transcription works](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/transcription); [AI-enhanced notes](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/ai-enhanced-notes)
- Otter: “Audio is processed in cloud infrastructure.” Summaries are regenerated from the stored transcript. — [Otter.ai Terms of Service](https://otter.ai/terms-of-service); [Set a custom Data Retention policy](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/19500988656279-Set-a-custom-Data-Retention-policy)
- Fireflies: meeting content is processed by vendors under a zero-retention rule, and Fireflies stores the transcript, summary, and recordings in the user’s account. — [Privacy Policy](https://fireflies.ai/privacy-policy)
- Wispr Flow: “Transcription always happens in the cloud.” — [Wispr Flow privacy](https://wisprflow.ai/privacy)
- Superwhisper: offline models are supported and are the ones that run well on Apple silicon; cloud models (the page names GPT, Claude, and Llama) are available and are what Intel Macs should use. — [Superwhisper](https://superwhisper.com)
- Screenpipe: local transcription and a local Ollama chat stay on device; a cloud model is a separate, user-enabled send of selected context. — [Privacy data flow](https://docs.screenpipe.com/privacy-data-flow)
- Recall’s snapshot analysis is local and does not use a cloud connection for that analysis. It is not a speech system. — [Privacy and control over your Recall experience](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/privacy/privacy-and-control-over-your-recall-experience)

### Inferences

- On-device in this market means the recognizer: whisper.cpp or Apple `SpeechTranscriber` / on-device Dictation. A product can be “private” and still be cloud if it streams audio and only promises deletion (Granola) or vendor zero-retention (Fireflies, Wispr’s third parties).
- Long-form meeting audio is explicitly in scope for Apple’s on-device `SpeechTranscriber` as of WWDC25. That removes the older assumption that OS speech is only short dictation. Hardware and language gates remain, and the October 2026 language list was not confirmed from Apple.
- Speaker diarization is the piece these on-device recognizers are not shown to include. whisper.cpp’s README, in the sections used here, documents VAD and a naive mic stream, not speaker labels. Apple’s SpeechTranscriber documentation describes text, alternatives, and time ranges, not speaker IDs. Otter’s enrollment store and Fireflies’ speaker labels are cloud product features.
- Note enhancement (summary, action items, “polished” prose, Granola’s grey text) is a second model. In Granola, Otter, Fireflies, and Wispr it is cloud. Superwhisper and Screenpipe document a local or user-chosen model for that second step (Llama / Ollama versus GPT or Claude). Apple’s Notes summaries are tied to Apple Intelligence in the WWDC talk; this pass did not confirm on-device versus Private Cloud Compute.

### Gaps

- This pass did not confirm a whisper.cpp diarization flag in the README sections read. Absence here is not a claim that no branch or flag exists.
- No measured speed, word-error, or memory figures are included. None of the primary pages reviewed published a benchmark that this review should repeat.
- Apple’s current `supportedLocales` for `SpeechTranscriber` on macOS as of 2 October 2026 was not fetched from a device or from an Apple language matrix.
- Whether Granola’s unnamed transcription provider could be swapped for an on-device engine is a product question, not something their docs answer.
