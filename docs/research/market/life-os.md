# Life-OS patterns: brief, mail, tasks, calendar

As of 2026-10-02. Products named below are the ones whose official docs were checked: Claude (desktop and web connectors), ChatGPT, Gemini Apps, Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Tasks, Superhuman Mail, Shortwave, Reclaim, and Motion. Claims are limited to pages retrieved for this note. Where a marketing page and a help article disagree, both are recorded.

## What do the strongest assistants ship for a daily brief, inbox triage, reply tracking, and calendar scheduling?

### Takeaway
Gemini is the only one of these assistants with a first-class daily brief that proactively mixes Gmail, Calendar, and chat. Claude and ChatGPT ship on-demand mail and calendar tools, and ChatGPT adds scheduled and Gmail-triggered tasks that can stand in for a brief. Purpose-built mail clients (Superhuman, Shortwave) still own triage and reply tracking; Google’s own Gmail and Calendar UIs own “offer times, then book.”

### Cited Findings

Daily brief

- Gemini Apps generates a Daily Brief that summarizes connected apps such as Gmail and Google Calendar, plus Gemini chats. It has two sections: “Top of mind” (timely items, primarily Gmail, Calendar, and chats) and “Looking ahead” (longer-term goals and suggested next steps from Personal Intelligence). The user can chat about an item, mark it complete or incomplete, dismiss it, and mark it helpful or not. — [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17077455?hl=en)
- Eligibility, fetched 2026-10-02: 18 or older and in the US; a personal Google Account (not work, school, or supervised); Personal Intelligence with Google Workspace connected and Memory on. English only. Surfaces listed on that help page: Gemini mobile app, gemini.google.com, and Gemini Live. The same page says the brief rolls out gradually and can be turned off under Settings & help → Personal Intelligence. — [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17077455?hl=en)
- A Gemini marketing page says the brief scans inbox, calendar, and tasks, and that the user can set reminders, open emails, draft replies, and schedule events from the brief. The help article above does not describe those controls. — [Gemini Daily Brief overview](https://gemini.google/overview/daily-brief/); contradicted in scope by [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17077455?hl=en)
- ChatGPT has no separate “daily brief” object in the help article retrieved. It does have one-time and recurring scheduled tasks, plus monitoring tasks that notify when something changes. Scheduled tasks can use connected apps including Gmail. Notifications can be push, email, or both. Active-task caps: 3 on Free and Go, 5 on Plus, 10 on Business and Edu, 15 on Pro and Enterprise. Free users are limited to once per day and to flexible windows (morning, afternoon, or night). Paid plans can run as often as hourly and can set an exact time. — [Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt) (page said “Updated: 17 hours ago” when fetched on 2026-10-02)
- Event-triggered ChatGPT tasks run in Work and can fire on a new Gmail message, optionally filtered by sender or subject. They require Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, or Edu (not Free or Go). They can run up to 30 times per hour and 720 times per day across all event-triggered tasks. If an action needs approval, the task pauses. The desktop app can show existing event-triggered tasks but cannot create or edit their trigger conditions; creation is on the web or supported mobile apps. — [Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt)
- Claude’s retrieved docs do not describe a proactive daily brief. The calendar connector’s published examples are on-demand: “What meetings do I have tomorrow and what should I prepare for each?” and “When do I have a free 2-hour block this week for deep work?” — [Google Calendar connector](https://claude.com/connectors/google-calendar)
- Claude Help says Workspace connectors (Gmail, Calendar, Drive) are available for all users on Claude and Claude Desktop, and that Claude answers with citations and links back to the emails, events, or documents used. — [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors) (page said “Updated today” when fetched on 2026-10-02)

Inbox triage

- Claude Gmail tools published on the connector page (added March 2026) include search_threads, get_message, get_thread, create_draft, list_drafts, reply, forward, send_message, label and unlabel (message and thread), create and delete labels, create and list filters, mark spam, and trash. Help text says Claude can search and read mail in natural language, draft with formatting, manage labels and threads, and list saved drafts. Attachment content is not available, only attachment metadata. Some advanced Gmail filters are unsupported. Large mailboxes vary in performance, and calls are subject to Google’s API quotas. — [Gmail connector](https://claude.com/connectors/gmail); [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)
- The Gmail connector’s own examples include summarizing mail from a team this week, drafting a reply, pulling mail related to a meeting, and asking whether unanswered mail from last week needs a follow-up. That last item is a prompt example, not a stored follow-up queue. — [Gmail connector](https://claude.com/connectors/gmail)
- Shortwave’s documented triage loop is: clear non-actionable mail (done, delete, or snooze), do anything under about two minutes immediately, and turn larger work into stars or todos, then return to it after the pass. Instant AI replies are inline suggestions for a short reply. Todos can be created from selected threads, with notes. — [The Shortwave Method](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/method/)
- Shortwave Inbox Splits are tabs defined by importance, labels, senders, or search. AI filters label mail from natural-language rules and can feed those splits. The assistant, asked to “organize my inbox,” recommends bulk archive, label, delete, and todo actions while the user stays in control. Documented prompts include “What are the most important emails in my inbox today?” and “Make a list of customers I emailed last week who haven’t responded.” — [Customize your Shortwave](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/customize-your-shortwave-settings/); [The Shortwave AI Assistant](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/ai-assistant/)
- Superhuman splits returned reminders into their own inbox section so they are not mixed with new Important or Other mail. Pending reminders live in a Reminders folder; fired ones appear in the Reminders split. — [Remind Me](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005666142733-Remind-Me)

Reply tracking

- Superhuman reminders default to “if no reply”: the thread returns to the top of the inbox only if nobody has responded. “Regardless” returns the thread even if someone replied. A reply before an “if no reply” reminder cancels that reminder; a “regardless” reminder still returns. — [Remind Me](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005666142733-Remind-Me)
- Superhuman Auto Reminders (Settings → Reminders) apply a default return time to one of: messages the model judges need a follow-up from the latest outgoing mail with no reply; every message with an external recipient; or no messages. — [Auto Reminders & Auto Drafts](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005658551053-Auto-Reminders-Auto-Drafts)
- Superhuman Read Statuses show time, device, and who opened a message. They work only for mail sent with Superhuman Mail, not with another client. Recent Opens (a live feed) is Business and Enterprise. There is no notification when someone opens a message; the user checks the sent message or the feed. Users can block known tracking pixels on incoming mail. — [Read Statuses and Recent Opens Feed](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005603745293-Read-Statuses-and-Recent-Opens-Feed)
- Team Reply Indicators in Superhuman show when a teammate is already drafting or has scheduled a send. — [Tracking Reads and Replies Together](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005816205581-Tracking-Reads-and-Replies-Together)
- Gmail’s 2018 product announcement said nudges would remind users to reply to mail they may have missed and to follow up when they had not received a response, on by default, with a settings toggle. No 2025–2026 help article confirming that nudge behavior was retrieved. — [Gmail will now remind you to respond (14 May 2018)](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2018/05/gmail-remind-respond.html)
- Google Tasks can carry links whose type includes “email,” but the task resource has no reply-state or thread-id field in the published schema. — [Tasks resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/tasks/reference/rest/v1/tasks#resource:-task)

Calendar scheduling

- Claude’s calendar connector tools are list_calendars, list_events, search_events, get_event, suggest_time, create_event, update_event, delete_event, and respond_to_event. Help text says Claude can view calendars the user can see (including shared), create, update, and delete events, find mutual availability, manage attendees, RSVP (accept, decline, tentative), and set up recurring meetings. The marketing examples include finding a time for named people and sending a 30-minute invite with a Meet link, moving a 1:1, and booking a room. — [Google Calendar connector](https://claude.com/connectors/google-calendar); [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)
- A Claude docs page still says, in search snippets, that Claude cannot create, modify, or delete events and cannot send invitations, and that this integration is on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise. That conflicts with the Help Center article updated on the fetch date and with the connector tool list. The docs page was not fetched in full. — [Claude docs, Google Calendar integration](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/google/calendar); contradicted by [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors) and [Google Calendar connector](https://claude.com/connectors/google-calendar)
- Inside Gmail, “Help me schedule” (Gemini) proposes times from the sender’s and guests’ availability. Default is 4 slots, maximum 20 guests. Green slots mean all guests are free; amber means a conflict and the user must pick manually. The user can change duration and time range, add a guest time zone, switch the card to plain text, or insert a booking page from an appointment schedule. Booking a slot creates a Calendar event and invites guests. Plan gate on the help page: Workspace Business Standard and Plus; Enterprise Starter, Standard, and Plus; Frontline Plus; Google AI Pro for Education; Google AI Pro and Ultra; Gemini Business and Enterprise (legacy). — [Suggest times to meet with Gemini in Gmail](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/16865189?hl=EN); [Schedule events in Gmail](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16865706?hl=en)
- A separate Gmail flow, “Propose times you’re free,” lets the sender pick slots on a grid. The event is not created until the recipient chooses. Recipients see times in their own time zone. This path is 1:1 only, and the help page limits it to Workspace Business Starter and personal Gmail accounts. Those accounts are pointed at this flow; other Workspace or Google AI plans are pointed at “Help me schedule.” — [Offer times to meet in a Gmail message](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16864784?hl=en); [Schedule events in Gmail](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16865706?hl=en)
- Any Gmail user can turn an existing thread into a Calendar invite; Gmail copies subject and body into the event and adds recipients as invitees. — [Schedule events in Gmail](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16865706?hl=en)
- Google Calendar appointment schedules are a booking page: buffer time, a daily booking cap, a choice of which calendars to check so slots do not conflict, co-hosts, Meet / in-person / phone / none, a booking form, and up to five reminder emails whose text cannot be edited. — [Create an appointment schedule](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/10729749)
- Superhuman Auto Drafts, when a mail needs a meeting, draft a reply that already contains the user’s available times, as soon as the mail arrives. That scheduling draft is listed alongside follow-up drafts and response drafts. — [Auto Reminders & Auto Drafts](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005658551053-Auto-Reminders-Auto-Drafts)
- The Calendar API’s free/busy query returns busy intervals for up to 50 calendars (calendarExpansionMax maximum 50) and expands a group to at most 100 calendars. It does not return event titles. Scopes include calendar.readonly, calendar, calendar.events.freebusy, and calendar.freebusy. — [Freebusy: query](https://developers.google.com/calendar/api/v3/reference/freebusy/query)
- No OpenAI help page retrieved for this note lists Google Calendar read or write tools. Secondary 2026 write-ups claim Calendar create-event and a June 2026 Gmail send that is web-only; those claims are not treated as confirmed here. Official ChatGPT pages that were fetched cover account connection, Gmail as an event source, and approval for actions that send or change external data. — [Connecting and managing app accounts](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001494-connecting-and-managing-app-accounts-in-chatgpt); [Connected apps in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11487775-connected-apps-in-chatgpt); [Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt)

### Inferences
- A “daily brief” in 2026 means a proactive, sectioned list (urgent vs later), sourced from mail and calendar, with complete / dismiss / feedback so the next brief changes. Chat-only assistants reach a weaker version by letting the user schedule “summarize my mail and calendar each morning.”
- Inbox triage that users can trust is recommend-then-confirm (Shortwave’s organize pass, Claude’s per-action approval), not silent archive or delete.
- “Reply tracking” splits into two products. Waiting-for-reply is thread state plus a timed return. Open tracking is a sender-side pixel that only works if that client sent the message. The second is not something Gmail or the Gmail API is documented here as providing.
- Scheduling that feels native is: read several people’s busy time, offer a few slots inside the draft, and create the event only when a person confirms. Google already does the last step inside Gmail. An assistant that only dumps a free/busy list into chat is behind that.

### Gaps
- Claude docs vs Help Center on whether calendar write is live. The stale-looking docs page was not opened in full.
- ChatGPT’s actual Google Calendar and Gmail tool lists (read vs create vs send, and which surfaces) were not on the official pages fetched. Secondary blogs disagree with each other and are unused as facts.
- Whether Gemini’s marketing claims (draft, schedule, and set a reminder from a brief card) match the shipped help-center UI.
- A current (2025–2026) Gmail help page for nudges. Only the 2018 launch post was found.
- Whether the public Calendar API can create or edit appointment schedules. Only the end-user help page was found.
- Spark, Apple Mail, and other clients were not reviewed.

## How do they handle recurring tasks, time blocking, and “propose then confirm” for sends?

### Takeaway
Recurring work and defended time blocks live in calendar products (Reclaim, Motion, and Calendar’s own RRULE), not in the Google Tasks API. Sends in Claude, and actions that send or mutate data in ChatGPT tasks, stop for a person to approve. Superhuman and Shortwave pre-write the reply and leave send to the user.

### Cited Findings

Recurring tasks

- The Google Tasks `Task` resource fields are kind, id, etag, title, updated, selfLink, parent, position, notes, status, due, completed, deleted, hidden, links, webViewLink, and assignmentInfo. There is no recurrence, repeat, or reminder field. `due` is an RFC 3339 timestamp, but “only date information is recorded; the time portion of the timestamp is discarded.” The docs say it is the day the task should be done or shown on the calendar grid, not a deadline, and that the scheduled time cannot be read or written. Status is only `needsAction` or `completed`. Title max 1024 characters; notes max 8192. Schema page last updated 2026-02-24 UTC. — [Tasks resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/tasks/reference/rest/v1/tasks#resource:-task)
- tasks.insert cannot create tasks assigned from Docs or Chat. A user can have up to 20,000 non-hidden tasks per list and 100,000 tasks total. Parent and previous-sibling are query parameters for position, not recurrence. — [tasks.insert](https://developers.google.com/tasks/reference/rest/v1/tasks/insert)
- The Tasks resource’s ContextType enum includes GMAIL, DOCUMENT, and SPACE, but assignmentInfo is described as output-only context for tasks assigned from Docs and Chat. — [Tasks resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/tasks/reference/rest/v1/tasks#resource:-task)
- Calendar events do recur. events.insert takes a `recurrence` list of RRULE, EXRULE, RDATE, and EXDATE lines per RFC 5545. DTSTART and DTEND are not allowed in that list; start and end are separate fields, and for a recurring event they are the first instance. The start time zone is required for recurring events and is the zone in which the rule expands. events.list hides non-exception instances unless `singleEvents=true`. Changing “this and following” is two calls: update the old rule with UNTIL (or COUNT), then insert a new series starting at the target instance. — [Recurring events](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/recurringevents); [Events: insert](https://developers.google.com/calendar/api/v3/reference/events/insert)
- Claude Help lists “set up recurring meetings” as a calendar connector capability. — [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)
- Motion’s public API creates a recurring task with a frequency, a duration (an integer greater than 0, or the string `REMINDER`), an ideal time of day (`HH:mm`) used when that time is free, a schedule the task must stay inside (default “Work Hours”), and a deadline type of `HARD` or `SOFT` (default SOFT). The date is trimmed to the start of the day. — [Motion API, create recurring task](https://docs.usemotion.com/api-reference/recurring-tasks/post)
- Reclaim Habits are flexible repeating blocks: the user sets frequency, duration, and time windows, and Reclaim places and moves them. Reclaim Tasks are one-off or deadline-bound work with an estimated duration, scheduled before the due date around meetings. Focus Time is a weekly hour goal of movable deep-work blocks with no single task attached. Work Habits and Tasks count toward the focus goal and are scheduled before generic focus. — [Habits vs. Tasks vs. Focus Time](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/11325700-habits-vs-tasks-vs-focus-time-when-to-use-each-in-reclaim); [Getting started with Reclaim](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/5224992-getting-started-with-reclaim)
- ChatGPT scheduled tasks recur on the plan limits above (daily window on Free; up to hourly on eligible paid plans). They are ChatGPT jobs, not Google Tasks. — [Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt)

Time blocking

- Reclaim’s model is continuous rescheduling. Habits, Tasks, Smart Meetings, Scheduling Links, and even ordinary Google or Outlook events can be tagged Critical (P1) through Low (P4). Higher priority can overbook lower-priority Reclaim events. Same priority: Smart Meetings, then Habits, then Tasks; tasks with the same priority go by soonest due date. A non-Reclaim event is overbooked only if the user lowers its priority in the Planner, and then only by a higher-priority scheduling link. Unless locked, an overbooked smart event moves within a few seconds. Users can auto-lock Smart Meetings, Habits, and Tasks at the start of the day or week so the near-term plan stops moving. A June 12, 2026 help article says task order can be weighted by due date, by work remaining, or by both (the default). Tasks and Habits can auto-start at the scheduled time or wait for a manual Start. — [How Reclaim manages your schedule automatically](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/6207587-how-reclaim-manages-your-schedule-automatically); [Priorities](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/8291694-how-reclaim-uses-priorities-to-intelligently-plan-your-workweek); [Scheduling settings](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/10491907-customize-your-scheduling-settings-for-reclaim-events)
- Reclaim also defends a no-meeting day and still lays Focus, Habits, and Tasks onto it. — [Getting started with Reclaim](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/5224992-getting-started-with-reclaim)
- Claude’s documented scheduling assist is prompted: suggest_time, then create_event, including a user asking for a free 2-hour block. Nothing retrieved describes a standing rule that keeps moving that block when a meeting lands on it. — [Google Calendar connector](https://claude.com/connectors/google-calendar)
- Motion’s recurring-task API is the closest public description retrieved of “put this on the calendar at an ideal time, inside working hours, and treat the deadline as hard or soft.” The help center for how the Motion product reshuffles a week was not fetched. — [Motion API, create recurring task](https://docs.usemotion.com/api-reference/recurring-tasks/post)

Propose then confirm for sends

- Claude can send, reply, and forward from Gmail. By default it asks for approval before each of those actions. On Team and Enterprise, owners can let members allow those actions to run without asking. The OAuth screen mentions send permission; Help says send still waits for explicit approval by default. The broader rule on the same page: by default every action on the user’s behalf needs explicit approval. — [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)
- Claude’s Gmail tool list separates create_draft, list_drafts, reply, forward, and send_message, so a draft can exist before a send. — [Gmail connector](https://claude.com/connectors/gmail)
- ChatGPT: “An action that sends a message or changes external data may require approval. If approval is required, the task pauses until you review it.” Workspace admins may control persistent permissions and which actions are allowed. Personal-account users manage their own app permissions. — [Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt)
- Superhuman Auto Drafts write follow-ups and replies in the user’s voice for the user to review, edit, and send. Follow-up drafts are added about an hour before a reminder (automatic or manual) returns. Response drafts are written when a mail that needs a reply arrives, including when the Respond auto-label is off. The follow-up and response toggles, and the “one click or edit and send” wording, are documented on the Business and Enterprise Auto Drafts pages. — [Auto Reminders & Auto Drafts](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005658551053-Auto-Reminders-Auto-Drafts); [Follow Up Faster](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005792082445-Follow-Up-Faster)
- Shortwave’s organize flow recommends archive, label, delete, and todo actions “while still staying fully in control at each step,” and instant replies are suggestions rather than an automatic send. — [The Shortwave AI Assistant](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/ai-assistant/); [The Shortwave Method](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/method/)
- Gmail API: users.drafts.send sends an existing draft to its To, Cc, and Bcc. When a draft is sent, the draft is deleted and a new message with the SENT label is returned. Scopes that can send a draft: `mail.google.com`, `gmail.modify`, or `gmail.compose`. users.messages.send can also send a raw message and additionally allows `gmail.send`. — [users.drafts.send](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.drafts/send); [Create and send draft emails](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/drafts); [users.messages.send](https://developers.google.com/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.messages/send)

### Inferences
- “Recurring tasks” in a Google-backed personal app cannot be stored as Google Tasks repeats. The durable options visible in these docs are a local recurrence rule that materializes the next dated task, or a Calendar event with an RRULE (which can hold a time of day, unlike Tasks).
- Time blocking that matches Reclaim is an app-owned scheduler: priority, duration, due date, working hours, and a lock window, writing ordinary calendar events and moving them when conflicts appear. Calendar will store the block; it will not defend it.
- Propose-then-confirm is the default safety pattern in 2026 for assistants that can send. The API shape is drafts.create (or drafts.update) for the proposal, then drafts.send only after a local confirm. `gmail.compose` is enough for that pair; `gmail.send` cannot create the draft side.

### Gaps
- Motion’s user-facing reschedule rules (what it moves, and whether the user confirms each move) were not opened; only the recurring-task API fields were.
- Whether Superhuman Auto Drafts on lower-than-Business plans include response drafts. The pages tie Auto Drafts to Business and Enterprise in one article and describe the same behaviors without repeating the plan gate in another.
- ChatGPT’s exact confirm UI for a Gmail send (always-ask vs remember-for-this-app) was not on the pages fetched, only that a send or external write may pause for approval.
- No source retrieved says Claude or ChatGPT will auto-send a follow-up when a reminder fires without a person approving that send.

## What is table stakes in 2026 for a personal app that already has Google Calendar, Gmail, and Tasks?

### Takeaway
Once mail, calendar, and tasks are connected, the expected layer is a morning view that mixes unread-or-waiting mail with today’s events, a triage pass that drafts but does not send, a waiting-for-reply list, and scheduling that offers real free/busy slots. Repeating tasks and real time blocks are expected by specialist apps, but they are not available as fields on Google Tasks, so they are not free just because Tasks is connected.

### Cited Findings
- Gemini treats “Gmail + Calendar + chats, split into do-today vs later, with complete and dismiss” as the brief. It requires a paid Google AI plan (Plus, Pro, or Ultra on the regional help variant; the US English help page says a Google AI subscription and Personal Intelligence). It is not offered on work or school accounts. — [Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17077455?hl=en); the Plus/Pro/Ultra wording is on the same article’s other locale: [Gemini Apps Help, en-KE](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17077455?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en-KE)
- Claude treats natural-language search, citations back to the source message or event, draft-then-approve send, mutual availability, and create/update/delete including recurring meetings as the connector feature set for every Claude user, including desktop. — [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)
- ChatGPT treats a connected Gmail account as something a scheduled or event-triggered task can read and, if the action needs it, pause on before sending or changing data. A morning mail check is therefore a user-authored task, capped by plan (5 active tasks on Plus; hourly only on paid plans). — [Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt)
- Shortwave’s documented baseline for a mail app with AI is splits, natural-language auto-labels, a one-pass triage (snooze / do now / todo), and inline reply suggestions. — [The Shortwave Method](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/method/); [Customize your Shortwave](https://www.shortwave.com/docs/guides/customize-your-shortwave-settings/)
- Superhuman’s documented baseline for follow-through is an if-no-reply reminder on the way out, an automatic version of that reminder, and a draft waiting when the reminder returns. Open tracking is extra and only for mail that client sent. — [Remind Me](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005666142733-Remind-Me); [Auto Reminders & Auto Drafts](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005658551053-Auto-Reminders-Auto-Drafts)
- Gmail’s own scheduling baseline, for personal accounts, is either a recipient-picks-a-slot card (1:1, event created on accept) or, on higher Google AI / Workspace plans, Gemini proposing about four slots from real availability, including a path that inserts an appointment-schedule link. — [Offer times to meet](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/16864784?hl=en); [Suggest times with Gemini in Gmail](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/16865189?hl=EN)
- Reclaim’s baseline for a calendar that also holds tasks is: estimated duration, a due date, a priority, working-hours windows, and automatic moves when meetings collide, with a lock so today stops churning. — [How Reclaim manages your schedule automatically](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/6207587-how-reclaim-manages-your-schedule-automatically)
- Connecting Tasks does not provide repeats or a time of day. `due` is a date, and the API will not store a clock time. — [Tasks resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/tasks/reference/rest/v1/tasks#resource:-task)
- Connecting Gmail does not provide open tracking. Superhuman states read statuses require sending through Superhuman. — [Read Statuses and Recent Opens Feed](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46005603745293-Read-Statuses-and-Recent-Opens-Feed)

### Inferences
- Table stakes for an app that already has the three Google connections, judged against these products: (1) a daily surface that lists today’s events and the mail that needs a reply or a follow-up, (2) drafts the user can edit, with send as a separate confirmed step, (3) reminders that cancel when a reply arrives, (4) suggested meeting times from free/busy, written as an invite only after confirmation, (5) citations or deep links back to the thread or event.
- Not table stakes, because the leading general assistants do not ship them as standing systems: pixel-level open tracking, Reclaim-style automatic overbooking, and a brief that runs on work accounts. Those are differentiators of Superhuman, Reclaim, and Gemini’s consumer brief respectively.
- Showing Google Tasks on a calendar grid is weaker than it looks. The API date has no time, so a “task at 3pm” has to be a calendar event or a local field, not `due`.

### Gaps
- No single 2026 survey or Google document defines “table stakes.” The list above is an inference from the products whose docs were read.
- Todoist, Sunsama, Akiflow, and Fantastical were not checked, so this note can understate what dedicated planners treat as normal.
- Whether Gmail nudges are still on by default in 2026 was not confirmed past the 2018 post.

## Which mechanisms are realistic for a single-user local app with its own OAuth client?

### Takeaway
A desktop OAuth client used by one test user can read and write Calendar, read and draft Gmail, and read and write Tasks without a security review, as long as the Cloud project stays in Testing. That mode expires refresh tokens every 7 days. Gmail send-and-read scopes are restricted, so a published app that stores or transmits mailbox data needs verification and a security assessment. The realistic feature set is local: draft then send, free/busy slot math, RRULE time blocks, and a local recurrence engine in front of date-only Tasks.

### Cited Findings

Auth shape

- Google documents an installed-application OAuth client (Desktop app, among others). The flow redirects a browser to Google, returns an authorization code, and exchanges it for an access token. A refresh token is optional in the overview’s installed-app section; a backend may hold it. The client secret for an installed app is embedded in the app and “is obviously not treated as a secret.” Incremental scope requests are recommended (ask for Calendar when the user adds a calendar action, not at first launch). Page last updated 2026-05-26 UTC. — [Using OAuth 2.0 to Access Google APIs](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2)
- A Cloud project whose consent screen is External and whose publishing status is Testing gets refresh tokens that expire in 7 days, unless the only scopes are a subset of name, email, and profile (`userinfo.email`, `userinfo.profile`, `openid`). Gmail, Calendar, and Tasks scopes are outside that subset, so the 7-day rule applies. — [Using OAuth 2.0 to Access Google APIs](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2)
- Other refresh-token deaths that matter here: the user revokes the app; the refresh token is unused for six months; the user changes their password and the token includes Gmail scopes; the account exceeds its live refresh-token cap. There is a limit of 100 refresh tokens per Google Account per OAuth client; the next one silently invalidates the oldest. — [Using OAuth 2.0 to Access Google APIs](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2)
- While status is Testing, verification is not required and the app is limited to Google accounts listed as test users. — [Restricted scope verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification)
- Service accounts are for calling APIs as the app, without user consent. They are not a substitute for a person connecting personal Gmail. The same overview says service accounts are not members of a Workspace domain and do not inherit domain policies. — [Using OAuth 2.0 to Access Google APIs](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2)

What the APIs will and will not do

- Gmail scopes classified as restricted include `mail.google.com` (full mailbox, including immediate permanent delete), `gmail.readonly`, `gmail.compose` (drafts and send), `gmail.insert`, `gmail.modify` (read, compose, and send, but not bypass-trash permanent delete), and `gmail.metadata` (headers and labels, no body). Restricted scopes need restricted-scope verification. If the app stores restricted-scope data on servers, or transmits it, it also needs a security assessment. Google’s own example of least privilege: an app that only sometimes sends mail should not request a scope that can read the whole mailbox. — [Choose Gmail API scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes)
- Draft-then-send is a supported API sequence. Creating or sending a draft needs `gmail.compose` (also satisfied by the wider restricted scopes). Sending a raw message without a draft also accepts `gmail.send`. A sent draft is deleted and replaced by a message labeled SENT. — [users.drafts.send](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.drafts/send); [Create and send draft emails](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/drafts); [users.messages.send](https://developers.google.com/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.messages/send)
- Free/busy for scheduling is `freeBusy.query`. It returns busy windows only, for at most 50 calendars per call. The app computes the open slots locally. — [Freebusy: query](https://developers.google.com/calendar/api/v3/reference/freebusy/query)
- Recurring meetings and recurring blocks are `events.insert` with an RFC 5545 RRULE. A time zone on start/end is required for the rule. Series edits that should apply “this and future” are a trim plus a new insert, not a single patch. — [Recurring events](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/recurringevents)
- Tasks a local client can actually store: title, notes, status, a date-only due, parent/child order, and completion time. Not a clock time, not a repeat rule, not a reminder. Inserts that try to create Docs/Chat-assigned tasks fail. Caps: 20,000 non-hidden tasks per list, 100,000 total. — [Tasks resource](https://developers.google.com/workspace/tasks/reference/rest/v1/tasks#resource:-task); [tasks.insert](https://developers.google.com/tasks/reference/rest/v1/tasks/insert)
- Claude’s connector limits are the same Google quotas, plus product limits a local app would not inherit: no Gmail attachment bytes, incomplete advanced filters, and mailbox size affecting latency. Claude also stores retrieved mail, calendar, and Drive data on Anthropic servers tied to the chat. A local app that never uploads those bodies does not take on that copy. — [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)
- Workspace admins can block a third-party OAuth client until it is marked Trusted (Claude documents this for “Claude for Google Drive,” with about 15 minutes of propagation). A personal Gmail account has no such admin; a work account might. — [Use Google Workspace connectors](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10166901-use-google-workspace-connectors)

### Inferences
- For one person and their own Cloud project, staying in Testing is the path that avoids restricted-scope verification. The cost is a refresh token that dies every 7 days, so the app must expect `invalid_grant` and send the user through the browser again. Publishing the consent screen to remove that expiry is a different project: Gmail’s restricted scopes then need verification, and a security assessment if mailbox data is stored or transmitted off the machine.
- Least-privilege set that still covers the table-stakes behaviors: `gmail.readonly` or `gmail.metadata` for triage and “no reply yet,” `gmail.compose` for draft and confirmed send, `calendar` (or a narrower calendar scope plus freebusy) for read, free/busy, and event write, and `tasks` for the task list. `gmail.modify` and `mail.google.com` are only needed for label/archive/trash or permanent delete. Asking for them up front fights Google’s least-privilege example and makes the password-change revocation rule apply to a fatter token.
- Waiting-for-reply can be computed locally from threads the app has read (last message is outbound, no later inbound). It does not need a special Gmail scope beyond read. Open tracking cannot be computed from any field in the docs retrieved here; copying Superhuman means sending through a channel that inserts a pixel, which is a product and policy choice, not an API response.
- A local time-blocker can create events on a dedicated calendar and move them with the Calendar API when free/busy changes. Nothing in the Calendar API will do the priority math. Polling free/busy is the mechanism documented here; push channels were not reviewed.
- Appointment-schedule booking pages and Gemini’s “Help me schedule” card are Gmail/Calendar UI features with plan gates. A local client can imitate the outcome (propose slots, create the event on confirm) via free/busy and events.insert. It should not assume the booking-page object is writable.
- Tasks recurrence belongs in the local app. On each completion, the app writes the next instance’s date-only `due`, or it writes an all-day or timed Calendar event instead when the user cares about an hour.

### Gaps
- The desktop loopback redirect URI rules (which ports, exact vs loopback) live on the iOS and Desktop Apps page, which was not fetched. Only the installed-app overview was.
- The maximum number of OAuth test users was not on the pages fetched. Do not assume a published figure.
- Calendar scope classification (sensitive vs restricted) was not fetched. Only Gmail scopes were confirmed restricted.
- Gmail `users.watch` and Calendar push channels were not fetched, so this note does not say whether a local app can get push instead of polling.
- No Google page retrieved says an unverified production app may use restricted Gmail scopes after the user clicks through a warning. Implementer READMEs claim that for Tasks; they are not used here as permission to do it for Gmail.
